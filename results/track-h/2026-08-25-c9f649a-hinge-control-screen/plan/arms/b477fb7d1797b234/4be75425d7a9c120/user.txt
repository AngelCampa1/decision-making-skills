Today is 2026-08-25.

Tobin Ellery runs Nettlefold Mill at Ardenshaw, one stone driven off a leat
taken from the Cowler Brook. The Ashgrove Bakers' Group has offered him a
twelve-month supply contract running 2026-10-01 to 2027-09-30 and wants an
answer by 2026-09-04. Ashgrove will not split the order between two mills, so
it is the whole order or none of it.

The contract:

- Ashgrove wants 40 tonnes of flour across the year, a quarter of it sifted
  white and the rest wholemeal. Sifted white is bolted after milling and the
  bolter takes off a fifth as bran, which Ashgrove will not take, so each
  tonne of sifted white needs 1.25 tonnes through the stone.
- Ashgrove buys its own grain and delivers it to the mill. Nettlefold is paid
  £310 a tonne of flour delivered, clear of what the extra milling days cost
  it. Ashgrove has traded thirty years and pays on delivery.
- Ashgrove takes delivery monthly to a schedule Tobin sets, and flour milled
  ahead into the mill's bins counts against it. The bins hold six months of
  the order.
- Ashgrove's bakers would have to re-source for the rest of the year if the
  mill came up short, so the contract sets a single agreed sum of £14,000,
  payable if Nettlefold delivers less than the full tonnage and whatever the
  size of the shortfall.
- Ashgrove takes flour only from a mill on the Bakers' Group assurance scheme,
  so joining for the contract year is a condition of signing. The certificate
  lapses each September.
- The scheme charges one fee for the year whatever tonnage passes the stone,
  and fixes it at its October meeting. It will not quote before then. It has
  charged between four and nine thousand pounds every year since it
  began, and says the contract year will fall in that range.
- Twelve months, and either side may walk at the end of it with no penalty.
  Ashgrove has not said whether it expects to come back for a second year.

The mill:

- The stone mills 0.36 tonnes on a full milling day.
- Tobin stands the Saturday market himself, so Saturdays are not milling days.
  He reckons on 250 working days in the contract year, after the Christmas
  fortnight and after stone dressing and repair.
- The Saturday market, Dunsmere Bakehouse and Vale End Stores take 32 tonnes
  through the stone across the year, all of it wholemeal. Tobin keeps all
  three under either course, at that volume, whatever grain does, and none of
  the three asks for a certificate.
- Before the weir went in, the leat dropped below milling flow on about
  seventy working days a year and the stone stood idle on each of them. The
  brook's record goes back thirty years and no year in it has passed eighty
  low days.
- The weir and its gauge were finished in March 2026. The gauge has recorded
  three low days since, in a June that was the wettest in a decade, and it has
  not yet seen a dry September.
- Nothing new is bought for this contract. The stone and the bolter are in
  place, and Tobin has ruled out buying plant or putting work out to another
  mill.
- Merrin Ellery has agreed to work the extra milling days at the day rate she
  is already on, with no notice owed either way.
- Nettlefold has no borrowing and about £40,000 put by, so the agreed sum
  would be a bad year rather than the end of one.
- Nettlefold buys its own grain for the market and shop trade and reprices
  that flour each January on what the grain cost. The 2026 harvest is being
  cut this week and the price is not yet set.

The two courses are SIGN and DECLINE.
