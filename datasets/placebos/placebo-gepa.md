# On deciding things well

People carry a decision around for a while before they ask anyone about it. By
the time a question gets spoken aloud it has usually been turned over in
private, put down, picked up again. Whatever comes back to them lands on top of
all that.

## The state a question arrives in

A question that is easy to answer rarely gets asked. What arrives is the residue
of a private argument the person has already had with themselves and could not
settle. Some of that argument shows in what they wrote. Most of it does not.

The shape of the writing carries something too. What they put first, what they
came back to twice, the part described in more detail than it seemed to need:
none of that is decoration.

## Attention before opinion

Forming a view early is comfortable and it costs something. Once a view exists,
everything read afterwards gets read as support for it or against it, and the
reading gets faster and worse. The remedy is dull and it works: finish reading
before settling on what you think.

None of this is quick. Slow reading feels like the part that is not yet work,
which is why it gets dropped first whenever there is a great deal to get
through, and dropping it is what makes the rest of the effort go astray.

## What people are actually asking for

Some people want a recommendation. Some want their own thinking handed back in a
shape they can look at from the outside. Some want permission, though they will
not put it that way. Some want to hear plainly that the thing they have been
dreading is the thing to do.

Those requests look alike on the page. Answering the wrong one produces a reply
that is accurate and useless.

## Holding a view loosely enough

Confidence is information. Stated, it can be weighed. Left implicit, it gets
inherited whole, and its worth becomes apparent only later.

There is an asymmetry here worth keeping in mind. Saying you are unsure costs a
little at the time you say it. Sounding sure when you are not costs later, at a
moment you will not be present for, to someone who is.

## Writing it down

Much of the work sits in the record rather than in the thinking. A thought kept
only in the head reorganises itself quietly, so that yesterday's doubt becomes
today's certainty with no memory of having moved.

A page kept for yourself needs no format and benefits from a little:

```
    the date
    what I think today
    what I thought before
    what moved
```

Nobody else reads it, so it can be blunt, and blunt is the whole value. A note
written to impress a future reader stops being useful the moment it starts being
careful.

Reading works the same way. A margin is a place to argue with the page:

```
    the passage              the objection
    the sentence as written  what it rests on
```

## Ordinary language

Plain words do more here than careful ones. A specialised term earns its place
where the everyday word would be less exact, and the everyday word is usually
exact enough.

Length is not care. A long answer can be a way of walking around the difficulty
without going into it, and the person who asked can generally tell which one
they got.

## Ending well

Put the thing that matters most where it will be found, and let the rest sit
beneath it. A summary of everything already said is a way of finishing without
ending. What a person carries away is smaller than what they were given, and
they keep whatever was easiest to hold.
