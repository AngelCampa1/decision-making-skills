# 2026-08-25 — Prediction: will Families B and C ceiling too?

Registered before the P3b control screens run, before any of the three repaired
instruments has produced a number, and before I have seen any repair land. The
predictions below are about **what a model does**, not about what a scorer
returns, so they survive the primaries still moving under repair.

Family A closed today at unaided J = 1.000 over 99 blind readings. The
generalisation drawn from it was that a scenario compact enough to fit one prompt
and answerable by one number is not, for a current model, hard. Families B and C
are compact and fit one prompt. They are not answerable by one number. **This
entry is the bet that the second half is what mattered, and it is a bet I am not
confident in.**

## What each screen asks

Per family, read the unaided arm before authoring anything at scale. `hinge` and
`cascade` have answer keys and a registered kill at unaided J at or above 0.70.
`council` has neither an answer key nor a base arm, so its ceiling analogue is
the opposite failure: a second-position rate indistinguishable from 0.5 means no
order effect exists and there is nothing for a procedure to fix.

## The predictions

**1. `hinge` will come in high and I expect it to fire the kill.** Identifying
which unknown would change the answer is threshold arithmetic plus one
counterfactual, and threshold arithmetic is precisely what nine readers just did
five times each without a slip. Point prediction: **crossed primary 0.75**, and I
would not be surprised by 0.9. If the kill is at 0.70 this fires.

The reason to run it anyway is that `hinge` is the one instrument whose failure
mode is *not* an arithmetic failure. A model can compute every constraint's slack
correctly and still answer the wrong question, and the review that broke `hinge`
showed the primary cannot currently tell a hinge test from a scan for the tightest
margin. So a high number here is ambiguous in a way a high number in Family A was
not, and the third arm registered during the repair is what disambiguates it.

**2. `cascade` is where I expect headroom, and it is the least confident
prediction here.** Point prediction: **unaided J 0.45**, below the kill.

The reasoning: the treatment and control differ by whether an ability is removed
or an event merely occurs, and that distinction has no arithmetic. Models
over-attribute consequence to whatever is vivid and nearby — the repair's own
fixture set has a false-alarm case built on exactly that. Over-attribution costs
specificity without touching sensitivity, which is the one shape that keeps a
matched contrast off its ceiling.

Against my own prediction: the v2 note expected specificity 1.0 and said so, and
the single blind control response it rested that on turned out not to exist as a
transcript. So the prior in the other direction has nothing behind it, which is a
reason to distrust it rather than a reason to trust mine.

**3. `council` will show a real order effect, and the run will fail to report
it.** Point predictions: **true second-position rate 0.60**, and **commit rate
0.70**.

Position dependence in language models is well attested and the `undefeated`
stratum is built to be the case where it bites hardest — when content does not
settle the question, order can. But the review found the reporting gate requires
`c > 0.5/ρ`, so at ρ = 0.60 a commit rate below 0.833 cannot produce a reportable
result at any sample size. At my predicted pair the run is refused. **If the
repair splits the bound so that `BALANCED` no longer enters it, this prediction
becomes reportable and that is the test of whether the repair mattered.**

**4. At least one of the three screens will be uninterpretable for a reason that
is not the model.** Every instrument here has now been rebuilt twice, and each
rebuild introduced a defect of the same family as the one it fixed. I am
registering this because it is the outcome the record most supports and the one I
would otherwise not write down.

## Where I expect to be wrong

The honest answer is prediction 2, and in the direction that would close the
track. Family A's closure was itself a lost bet — I predicted the unaided arm
would not be unanimous and it was unanimous 9 of 9 — and the mechanism that beat
me was not the one under test. If `cascade` also ceilings, the reading available
is not that three constructs failed but that **single-call scale is the wrong
scale**, and the successor is the volume and delegation venue rather than a fourth
instrument.

The reading I would resist, and want on record before the numbers arrive: a high
unaided J on `hinge` and `cascade` says these two items are easy for a current
model. It does not say the procedures are useless, because nothing here has tested
them at volume, over long context, under delegation, or across a conversation, and
the failures the six procedures describe mostly do not happen in one call.

## What is not being predicted

No prediction about effect size for any skill arm. No `on` record exists anywhere
in this project, so a number would be invention rather than estimate. P3b measures
whether there is anything for a procedure to help with. That question comes first
and it is the one that closed five venues before this one.
