# 2026-08-25 — prediction: can a `ledger` item be repaired off the ceiling?

Registered before the run. The result it will be read against is
[the ninety-reading probe](2026-08-25-the-ledger-venue-ceilings-at-ninety-readings.md),
where `ledger` v1 closed on both its kills: unaided J = 1.000, and 3 of 5
triplets cut.

**This does not reopen that result.** `ledger` v1 is closed and stays closed. The
question here is different and it is new: the reviewers who cut those items also
named the mechanism and two repairs for it, and nobody has tested whether the
repairs work. If they do not, Family A closes entirely rather than dropping to
`timing`, and that is a stronger and more useful finding than "one construct of
three did not work."

## The mechanism, as three independent reviewers described it

Volume buys **retrieval** and not **discrimination**. A reader must scan all ten
siblings to find which is slowest or which body is the registered practice. But
the rule sentence is single-input and exclusive — *"a lot's route is set by the
off-type count in the final field inspection report for the field it was grown
in"* — so reading one sentence eliminates seven siblings at a stroke, and reading
one more eliminates two of the remaining three. Effective width goes 10 → 3 → 1
in two string matches, with no dates and no arithmetic at either step.

The general form, from the L04 review: **skeleton identity and the same-alarm
requirement are structurally incompatible whenever the swapped proper noun is the
entity the rule sentence selects on, unless resolving that entity requires work.**

## The two repairs, both preserving skeleton identity

**R-a, indirect binding.** The base identifies the governed entity by a property
that has to be resolved through the sibling set, never by name in the core block.
Today L04 says *"CS-6053 is the whole of the Field 4 crop"* in the core, so the
resolution costs one lookup.

**R-b, multi-input rule.** The rule sentence routes on two quantities rather than
one, so every sibling carrying either becomes live and the one-sentence filter
stops working.

Both leave the two inserts character-identical, which is the constraint that
killed the repair the L04 author was offered and correctly refused.

## What will be run

One triplet authored under R-a **and** R-b together, in a domain none of L01–L05
used. Then the same probe that closed v1, unchanged: three arms, three blind
instances each, bare contract, no arithmetic demand, no fork warning. **Nine
calls.** The estimator, the denominator and the scorer are the ones already on
disk in `predict_ceiling.py`, reading arms rather than readings.

Running the bare arm first and the G1 brief second, rather than the other way
round, because v1 showed the expensive gate was measuring the cheap thing.

## Predictions

1. **The unaided arm will not be unanimous on all three arms.** This is the whole
   bet. v1 went 15 of 15; anything short of 3 of 3 here is movement.
2. **If it fails to be unanimous, it will fail on the matched arm, not the base.**
   R-a and R-b both add resolution work to identifying the governed entity, which
   is what the matched arm turns on. A base that stops being recoverable means the
   item is under-specified rather than hard, and that is a cut, not a success.
3. **Effective sibling width will rise above 3 and stay below 10.** Ten was always
   an advertised figure; I do not expect a rule sentence that makes all ten live.
4. **The item will be harder to author, and the authoring cost is part of the
   result.** If R-a and R-b can only be satisfied by making the matched fact
   subtle, they have collapsed into the `fit` mechanism whose yield is one in
   five, and the repair is not a repair.

## The kill

**If the unaided arm reads 3 of 3 unanimous and equal to key, Family A closes
entirely.** Not `ledger` — Family A. Two constructs, two dials, two ceilings, and
a third dial that only works by becoming the dial that does not scale. The
successor is already written: Families B and C carry the track, and the finding
is that scalar elicitation over an authored corpus does not produce headroom at
single-call scale.

A single triplet cannot support that on its own, and I am not going to pretend
otherwise: n = 1 with three instances is a screen, not an estimate. What it can
do is tell me whether to spend the next 200 authoring calls, and that is the
decision actually in front of me.

## Where I expect to be wrong

Prediction 1 is the one I would bet against myself on. Every reviewer who
described the mechanism also said the items were *sound* — L02 survived eight
objections, L03 six. The ceiling did not come from bad authoring, and there is a
real chance it does not come from the binding structure either, but from
something simpler that neither the reviewers nor I have named: that a scenario
compact enough to fit one prompt and answerable by one number is, for a current
model, not hard. If that is true, R-a and R-b will change nothing measurable and
the kill fires on a repair that was never going to matter.
