\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What a ratchet without a control can and cannot tell you}

Both engines improved a validation score and both accepted on it. Neither
improvement survived a placebo. That gap has a mechanical explanation which does
not require either engine to be badly built.

An accept-if-strictly-better rule performs one comparison per proposal and keeps
every winner. Over a run of a few hundred rollouts it therefore performs a few
hundred implicit tests, reports none of them, and has no way to ask whether a
difference could have arisen by chance. Our own repeat measurement puts the
standard deviation of a 21-item validation score at about 1.2 items, and both
winners sit three items above their seed. A ratchet in that regime is a device
for selecting noise, and it will select noise with perfect reliability given
enough proposals.

The fix is cheap and neither engine has it: hold out items the ratchet cannot
see, and require a candidate to beat the incumbent on those before accepting.
Nothing about either architecture prevents it.

\subsection{Memorisation is the reachable optimum, not a bug}

Both winners wrote training answers into the skill body, by different routes,
onto near-disjoint parts of the corpus. Neither engine was asked to. Both were
asked to maximise a score on items they could see, and transcribing the answers
is the best available way to do that.

This has a design consequence for anyone evaluating these engines. A holdout
that varies only the random seed of a template will score that transcription as
generalisation, because the transcribed rule is correct for every seed of the
template it came from. Our original plan was exactly such a holdout, and a probe
caught it before the study rather than after. \textbf{Split by template, or the
evaluation rewards the failure it exists to detect.}

\subsection{The placebo moved the model, which is the more useful finding}

The most actionable number in \Cref{tab:seen} is not either engine's. It is the
placebo at \NUM{\accSeenPlacebo} against \NUM{\accSeenOff} for an empty prompt.
A document of the right length and shape, whose content carries no method,
outperformed both the skill we wrote and one of the two evolved winners.

Read one way this undercuts the premise of writing skills at all, and we follow
that honestly: on this corpus and this model, a substantial part of what a skill
does is occupy the system prompt with structured text. Read another way it is a
warning about every published skill result that lacks this arm, which is nearly
all of them. Either reading requires the arm.

\subsection{Where this leaves the SkillsBench and SkillOpt disagreement}

Our result is closer to SkillsBench's null than to SkillOpt's $+23.5$ on GPT-5.5
in direct chat, and we
report that with the scope it deserves. We tested one corpus, one target model
at 1.7B parameters, and one task family with computable ground truth. SkillOpt
evaluates inside agent harnesses on multi-step work, which is a different regime
in a way that could plausibly matter: an evolved skill that fixes a formatting
pathology is worth little on a single forced choice and could be worth a great
deal across a long trajectory.

The one gain we found that exceeded measurement error points that way. Half of
the seed skill's failures were generations that ran to the token cap without
concluding, and both winners took those to zero. That is a real effect, larger
than the noise, and it is a formatting fix. On a single-answer benchmark it is
worth a few points. In an agent loop it might be worth much more, and nothing
here measures that.

\subsection{What a verdict is worth}

The claim we are entitled to is that on \NUM{\studyModel}, on this corpus, under
these controls, neither engine's output beat a document-shaped placebo, and one
scored below an empty prompt. Harness variance exceeds model variance by roughly
$7.8\times$~\citep{harnessdisclosure2026}, so transfer to another scaffold
should not be assumed. That is the same warning that literature issues about everyone
else's numbers, and we apply it to ours.

\subsection{Negative results, at the same prominence}

Reported here rather than in a footnote, because publishing the nulls is what
stops a reader discounting anything else on the page.

The skill we wrote and ship lost to a placebo on both item sets. Three
registered predictions about corpus construction were falsified in two days
(\Cref{sec:ceiling}). Three of our own ten templates measure nothing
(\Cref{sec:signal}). One earlier run in this project is void, refused on parse
rate before any prediction was scored. And the headline experiment could not be
replicated at a tier that would carry a verdict, for a reason that is a property
of our corpus rather than of the engines.
