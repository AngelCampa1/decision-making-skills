\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What a ratchet without a control can and cannot tell you}

Both engines improved a validation score and both accepted on it. Neither
improvement survived a placebo. That gap has a mechanical explanation which does
not require either engine to be badly built.

An accept-if-strictly-better rule performs one comparison per proposal and keeps
every winner. Over a run of a few hundred rollouts it therefore performs a few
hundred implicit tests, reports none of them, and has no way to ask whether a
difference could have arisen by chance. SkillOpt accepted its winner on 21 of 21
validation items against a seed's 18, with no interval on either. GEPA accepted
its winner on three items and never scored it on the pool at all. A ratchet in
that regime is a device for selecting noise, and it will select noise with
perfect reliability given enough proposals.

The fix is cheap and neither engine has it: hold out items the ratchet cannot
see, and require a candidate to beat the incumbent on those before accepting.
Nothing about either architecture prevents it.

\subsection{Memorisation is the reachable optimum, not a bug}

Both winners wrote training-item constants into the skill body, and both landed
on the same training template with different numbers. Neither engine was asked
to. Both were asked to maximise a score on items they could see, and
transcribing what they saw is the best available way to do that.

This has a design consequence for anyone evaluating these engines. A holdout
that varies only the random seed of a template will score transcribed
template-specific material as generalisation, because the transcription still
fits the template it came from. Our original plan was exactly such a holdout,
and a probe caught it before the study rather than after. \textbf{Split by
template, or the evaluation rewards the failure it exists to detect.}

Whether the transcription paid is a question this design cannot answer, which
is worth separating from the lesson. GEPA lifted constants from two training
templates, \texttt{rel-007-capacity-scale} and
\texttt{rel-008-contract-renew}; SkillOpt lifted from the second alone. Neither
appears in the unseen set, so no unseen number can speak to what they cost.

Inside the seen set the two engines point opposite ways. GEPA reads
\NUM{\accSeenRelZeroZeroEightContractRenewGepa} and
\NUM{\accSeenRelZeroZeroSevenCapacityScaleGepa} on the templates its constants
came from, against an empty prompt's
\NUM{\accSeenRelZeroZeroEightContractRenewOff} and
\NUM{\accSeenRelZeroZeroSevenCapacityScaleOff}, and its unseen deficit is on a
template none of them touch. SkillOpt reads
\NUM{\accSeenRelZeroZeroEightContractRenewSkillopt} on
\texttt{rel-008-contract-renew}, and that single template carries
\NUM{\netSeenRelZeroZeroEightContractRenewSkillopt} of the net items behind its
headline \NUM{\effectSeenSkillopt} (\Cref{sec:whatengines}).

So the second largest contribution to the largest gain in this study sits on the
one template the winning engine memorised a threshold for. We are not entitled
to call that the cause: it is one template, one engine, an unregistered
comparison, and \texttt{rel-005-security-patch} carries
\NUM{\netSeenRelZeroZeroFiveSecurityPatchSkillopt} with no constant lifted from
it at all. What we are entitled to say is that a corpus with a fixed
threshold would have rewarded the same behaviour outright, this one rewarded it
on the template it could, and no design we ran can separate the two.

\subsection{The placebo moved the model, which is the more useful finding}

The most actionable number in \Cref{tab:seen} is not either engine's. It is the
placebo at \NUM{\accSeenPlacebo} against \NUM{\accSeenOff} for an empty prompt.
A document of the right length and shape, whose content carries no method,
outperformed the skill we wrote, and on the unseen set it outperformed GEPA's
winner too.

Read one way this undercuts the premise of writing skills at all, and we follow
that honestly: on this corpus and this model, a substantial part of what a skill
does is occupy the system prompt with structured text. Read another way it is a
warning about every published skill result that lacks this arm, which is nearly
all of them. Either reading requires the arm.

\subsection{Where this leaves the SkillsBench and SkillOpt disagreement}

Our result is closer to SkillsBench's null than to SkillOpt's $+23.5$ on GPT-5.5
in direct chat~\citep{skillopt2026}, and we
report that with the scope it deserves. We tested one corpus, one target model
at 1.7B parameters, and one task family with computable ground truth. SkillOpt
evaluates inside agent harnesses on multi-step work, which is a different regime
in a way that could plausibly matter: an evolved skill that fixes a formatting
pathology is worth little on a single forced choice and could be worth a great
deal across a long trajectory.

The largest thing this run moved points that way, though not where we first
looked for it. Nearly every unreadable answer from the seed skill and the
placebo is a generation that ran to the output cap without concluding, and the
placebo produces the most of any arm (\Cref{sec:parsefail}). On a single forced
choice that is worth a few points and reads as noise. In an agent loop a model
that will not stop is a different kind of failure, and nothing here measures it.
An earlier draft credited the winners with fixing this; one does and one does
not, and the arm that provokes it hardest is a document with no content.

\subsection{What a verdict is worth}

The claim we are entitled to is that on \NUM{\studyModel}, on this corpus, under
these controls, neither engine's output beat a document-shaped placebo, and one
scored below an empty prompt. Harness variance exceeds model variance by roughly
$7.8\times$~\citep{harnessdisclosure2026}, so transfer to another scaffold
should not be assumed. That is the same warning that literature issues about everyone
else's numbers, and we apply it to ours.

\subsection{Two lines of arithmetic that would have stopped this study}

We ran a pre-registered five-arm study with a placebo, a holdout minted after
the winners were frozen, an A/A control, and a hash-locked registration verified
by git ancestry. It still could not have answered its own question, and the two
checks that would have shown that take a pocket calculator and a registration.

The first is the $p$-floor. Fix the unit of analysis, count the clusters, and a
one-sided exact test over $k$ of them cannot return a $p$ below $2^{-k}$. Our
unseen set is \NUM{\templatesUnseen} templates, so the floor is
\NUM{\floorUnseen}, and no outcome would have cleared the family's
$\alpha = \NUM{\studyAlpha}$, let alone Holm's tightest step of
\NUM{\mdeAlpha}.
The second is the minimum detectable effect, which our registration did compute
and then computed at a design effect of 1.0 for a corpus built from templates.
At the \NUM{\protocolDesignEffect} our own protocol specifies, the detectable
effect is \NUM{\mdeClusteredUnseen} against a largest observed gain of
\NUM{\effectSeenSkillopt}.

Neither check needs data. Both need someone to name the unit of analysis before
the run and then use the same unit afterwards, which is exactly what we failed
to do: our method section matched on items, our limitations section said
templates were the unit, and nothing reconciled them until the run was over.
That is a cheap failure to inherit and a cheap one to avoid, and it is the piece
of this paper we would most want a reader of the skill optimisation literature
to take away. A null from an underpowered design is not evidence of absence. A
null from a design with a $p$-floor above its own $\alpha$ is not evidence of
anything.

\subsection{Negative results}

The skill we wrote and ship lost to a placebo on both item sets. Three
registered predictions about corpus construction were falsified inside a day
(\Cref{sec:ceiling}). Three of our own ten templates measure nothing
(\Cref{sec:signal}). One earlier run in this project is void, refused on parse
rate before any prediction was scored. And the headline experiment could not be
replicated at a tier that would carry a verdict, for a reason that is a property
of our corpus, not of the engines.
