\textbf{No arm rejects on either set.} The strongest showing in the study is
SkillOpt's winner on the seen set at $\NUM{\effectSeenSkillopt}$, raw
$p = \NUM{\pSeenSkillopt}$, which Holm takes to $q = \NUM{\qSeenSkillopt}$. On

$p = \NUM{\pUnseenSkillopt}$.

Neither table carries an interval on its accuracy effect, and it should. The
run's analysis wrote the paired test and the effect and not a bootstrap around
it, and \texttt{de figures} renders that file rather than reanalysing it, so
adding one here would mean the paper and the published run disagreeing about the
same comparison. \Cref{tab:signal} does carry cluster-bootstrap intervals over
templates, on the decomposition and not on accuracy, which is where the
width of ten clusters is visible. The gap is on the checklist as one.

Three readings deserve stating and not leaving in the table.

\paragraph{GEPA's winner is worse than writing nothing, on one template of
three.} It scores \NUM{\accUnseenGepa} on unseen scenarios against
\NUM{\accUnseenOff} for an empty prompt and \NUM{\accUnseenPlacebo} for the
placebo, losing \NUM{\lossesUnseenGepa} items to the placebo's
\NUM{\winsUnseenGepa}. It is the arm furthest below \texttt{off} on that set; the seed skill is also
below it, at \NUM{\accUnseenOn}.

That aggregate is one template. On \texttt{rel-001-vendor-outage} GEPA reads
\NUM{\accUnseenRelZeroZeroOneVendorOutageGepa} against \texttt{off}'s
\NUM{\accUnseenRelZeroZeroOneVendorOutageOff}, a gap of
\NUM{\gepaLessUnseenRelZeroZeroOneVendorOutageOff}. Drop that one template and GEPA
becomes the \emph{best} unseen arm, at
\NUM{\accWithoutUnseenRelZeroZeroOneVendorOutageGepa} against \texttt{off}'s
\NUM{\accWithoutUnseenRelZeroZeroOneVendorOutageOff} and the placebo's
\NUM{\accWithoutUnseenRelZeroZeroOneVendorOutagePlacebo}. With
\NUM{\templatesUnseen} unseen templates a directional claim about the set is not
available either way, which is what \Cref{sec:clustered} reports as a floor.

The mechanism is visible in the same records. On \texttt{rel-001} GEPA gave one
answer to a share \NUM{\constantShareUnseenRelZeroZeroOneVendorOutageGepa} of
the \NUM{\parsedCountUnseenRelZeroZeroOneVendorOutageGepa} items it parsed at
all, against a balanced key: sensitivity 1, specificity 0, and an informedness
of exactly zero, which is the only such cell in the study and is already
printed in \Cref{tab:templates}. Its parse rate there is
\NUM{\parseRateUnseenRelZeroZeroOneVendorOutageGepa} against
\NUM{\parseRateUnseenRelZeroZeroOneVendorOutagePlacebo} for the placebo on the
same items. \Cref{sec:whatengines} describes the body.

\paragraph{The hand-written skill also fails to beat the placebo.} The
\texttt{on} arm reads \NUM{\accUnseenOn} unseen and \NUM{\accSeenOn} seen,
below the placebo on both. We wrote that skill and we ship it.

\paragraph{The placebo is not inert.} It reads \NUM{\accSeenPlacebo} on the
seen set against \NUM{\accSeenOff} for an empty prompt. The placebo is the
control and so has no row in the comparison column of \Cref{tab:seen}; that gap
is wider than every effect in it. It is not the widest gap over an empty prompt
in the table: SkillOpt's winner sits further above \texttt{off} at
\NUM{\accSeenSkillopt} and GEPA's is above the placebo too at
\NUM{\accSeenGepa}. What neither does is clear the placebo after correction. A structured document of the right length and shape, whose content
carries no method, moved this model. Any evaluation without such an arm would
have attributed that movement to whatever text it happened to be testing.

\subsection{An off-key answer is scored as a wrong answer, and it is not random}
\label{sec:parsefail}

Every arm's unreadable answers are scored wrong. That is the study's convention
and it is defensible, but it makes accuracy a sum of decision quality and format
compliance, and on the unseen set the arms differ on the second by more than
any effect here.

Two failure modes exist and the arms split across them. \texttt{gepa} fails
almost entirely by naming an option not on the menu and \texttt{off} mostly
does; \texttt{on}, \texttt{placebo} and \texttt{skillopt} fail almost entirely
by producing no answer line at all. A content-free or seed-derived document in
the system prompt converts off-key answers into missing ones. GEPA's winner does
the reverse and carries more off-key answers than an empty prompt, and nothing
in this study explains why: its body is gone (\Cref{sec:limitations}) and the
records carry no trace of it.

The missing ones have a mechanism, and it is not a property of the document's
content. A generation that stops exactly at the \NUM{\outputCap}-token output
cap did not choose to stop, and counting those per arm gives \NUM{\atCapOff} for
an empty prompt, \NUM{\atCapOn} for the seed skill and \NUM{\atCapPlacebo} for
the placebo, against \NUM{\unparsedOff}, \NUM{\unparsedOn} and
\NUM{\unparsedPlacebo} unreadable answers in total. \textbf{The arm with the most
runaway generations in this study is the one whose document says nothing.}
Putting text in the system prompt makes a 1.7B model talk itself out of
concluding. Nothing here ranks length against content: the shortest non-empty
body produces the most runaways and the longest produces fewer than the seed
skill, so the ordering is not length. An earlier draft
of this paper reported the opposite: that runaways were a seed skill pathology
both evolved winners fixed. GEPA's winner does suppress them, to
\NUM{\atCapGepa}, while carrying the most unreadable answers of any arm at
\NUM{\unparsedGepa}; SkillOpt's does not, at \NUM{\atCapSkillopt}.

\textbf{Parse failure is selective on the answer key.} The key on
\texttt{rel-001-vendor-outage} is balanced,
\NUM{\countOnUnseenRelZeroZeroOneVendorOutageGepaWait} items each way. GEPA
fails to produce a readable answer on
\NUM{\failOnUnseenRelZeroZeroOneVendorOutageGepaWait} of the items whose correct
answer is \texttt{wait} and on
\NUM{\failOnUnseenRelZeroZeroOneVendorOutageGepaFileSlaClaim} of the items whose
answer is \texttt{file\_sla\_claim}. When the right answer is to hold back, it
neither holds back nor abstains: it answers off-key. The same shape appears for
\texttt{off} on \texttt{rel-003-oncall-escalate}.

That has a consequence for any repair. Restricting to items both arms parsed
removes exactly the items an arm systematically fails, so it conditions on a
post-treatment variable that correlates with the key. We report the restriction
because it moves things — SkillOpt's unseen advantage over the placebo becomes
\NUM{\restrictedUnseenSkillopt} on a
\NUM{\restrictedWinsUnseenSkillopt}--\NUM{\restrictedLossesUnseenSkillopt} split,
and GEPA's seen comparison goes from $p = \NUM{\pSeenGepa}$ to
$\NUM{\restrictedPSeenGepa}$ — and we do not offer it as the corrected analysis.
Both readings are biased, in opposite directions, and this design cannot say
which is nearer.

\subsection{The same comparisons at the template level}
\label{sec:clustered}

\Cref{tab:unseen,tab:seen} treat items as independent. \Cref{sec:method} says
why they are not. Summing to one net difference per template and exchanging
those signs instead gives the test at the unit the design has.

\begin{tabular}{llrrr}
\toprule
set & arm & McNemar $p$ & clustered $p$ & clusters \\
\midrule
unseen & \texttt{on} & \NUM{\pUnseenOn} & \NUM{\clusteredUnseenOn} & \NUM{\clustersUnseenOn} \\
unseen & \texttt{gepa} & \NUM{\pUnseenGepa} & \NUM{\clusteredUnseenGepa} & \NUM{\clustersUnseenGepa} \\

\end{center}

Nothing rejects under either unit, so the paper's conclusion does not turn on
the choice. What the clustered column changes is how close anything came.
SkillOpt's seen comparison, the study's best showing at
$p = \NUM{\pSeenSkillopt}$ item-matched, reads
\NUM{\clusteredSeenSkillopt} clustered. It is one template: the net difference
is carried by \texttt{rel-005-security-patch} and the remaining six templates
sum against it.

\paragraph{The unseen set could not have rejected.} With $k$ templates a
one-sided sign-flip cannot return below $2^{-k}$, because the observed sign
vector is itself one of the $2^k$ assignments and so always falls inside its own
tail. Equality holds only when it also maximises the statistic. The unseen set has
\NUM{\templatesUnseen} templates, so its floor is \NUM{\floorUnseen} —
above the family's $\alpha = \NUM{\studyAlpha}$ before Holm and further above
Holm's tightest step of \NUM{\mdeAlpha} after. \emph{No outcome
of the three unseen comparisons could have been significant.} The seen set has
\NUM{\templatesSeen} templates and a design floor of \NUM{\floorSeen}, which
leaves room. The realised floor is higher, because a template that nets zero
drops out of the test: the seen comparisons ran on
\NUM{\clustersSeenSkillopt} and \NUM{\clustersSeenOn} live clusters, floors of
\NUM{\clusteredFloorSeenSkillopt} and \NUM{\clusteredFloorSeenOn}, and the
second is already above Holm's tightest $\alpha = \NUM{\mdeAlpha}$.

This is a property of the design and not of the data. It was computable when the
sets were drawn, and we did not compute it. \Cref{sec:limitations} carries what
that costs the registered predictions.

\subsection{Controls}

\paragraph{A/A: \NUM{\aaPairs} of \NUM{\aaPairs} identical.} The placebo scored
a second time over every item returned \NUM{\aaDisagreements} disagreements,
both passes at \NUM{\aaAccuracy}, $p = \NUM{\aaP}$. It bounds the drift between
blocks over the stretch it covers. It does not license reading every difference
in \Cref{tab:unseen,tab:seen} as prompt-caused: the pass repeats one arm, and
the arms ran in blocks (\Cref{sec:limitations}).

The pass ran into its own checkpoint so that resume could not skip it and report
perfect agreement by construction. It is also only trustworthy because of a
finding from hours earlier: two items answered deterministically to whether the
model had just been reloaded, and pinning residency closed it. A venue treated
as deterministic without measuring it would have put that artifact inside this
control.

\paragraph{Falsifier.} The \texttt{off} arm returns \NUM{\accUnseenOff} unseen
and \NUM{\accSeenOff} seen, so a non-zero score was reachable in every arm and
the scorer reads the same object in each.

\subsection{What the engines actually produced}
\label{sec:whatengines}

The scores above are the answer to the registered question. What the winners
contain is a separate finding and does not depend on a score.

\textbf{Both of the winners we tested had written training-item constants into
the skill body, and both engines accepted them on evidence too thin to have
noticed.}

GEPA's winner carries a rules table asserting renewal at 61\% utilisation and
capacity headroom at 37\%, and a worked example built on the numbers 2818 and
2032. Every one of those is a value from a single training item. The catch is
that \texttt{utilisation\_floor} is drawn fresh per item from the integer range
\NUM{\rangeRelZeroZeroEightContractRenewUtilisationfloorLow} to
\NUM{\rangeRelZeroZeroEightContractRenewUtilisationfloorHigh} and stated in each
item's Background, so a body carrying 61\% asserts
a threshold its own prompt contradicts on almost every item it meets.

Whether that cost anything is a question this split cannot answer. Both
constants belong to \texttt{rel-008-contract-renew} and
\texttt{rel-007-capacity-scale}, both are training templates, and neither is in
the unseen set. On the two templates concerned GEPA reads
\NUM{\accSeenRelZeroZeroEightContractRenewGepa} and
\NUM{\accSeenRelZeroZeroSevenCapacityScaleGepa} against an empty prompt's
\NUM{\accSeenRelZeroZeroEightContractRenewOff} and
\NUM{\accSeenRelZeroZeroSevenCapacityScaleOff}, at or above it. Its unseen
collapse is elsewhere, on \texttt{rel-001-vendor-outage}, which none of the
constants touch.

SkillOpt is the harder case and we report it against ourselves. On
\texttt{rel-008-contract-renew}, the one template it lifted a constant from, it
reads \NUM{\accSeenRelZeroZeroEightContractRenewSkillopt} against
\NUM{\accSeenRelZeroZeroEightContractRenewOff} for an empty prompt, and that
template carries \NUM{\netSeenRelZeroZeroEightContractRenewSkillopt} of the
net items behind \NUM{\effectSeenSkillopt}. That net is a signed sum over
templates and not a partition, so this is a share of the total and not of the
positives: \texttt{rel-005-security-patch} carries
\NUM{\netSeenRelZeroZeroFiveSecurityPatchSkillopt} with no constant lifted from
it, and the other five templates sum against both. What is left is that the second
largest contribution to the study's largest gain sits on the one template its
winner memorised a threshold for. One template, one engine, an unregistered
comparison: a fact and not a finding.

SkillOpt's winner carries 63\% for \texttt{rel-008-contract-renew} with the
arithmetic $213/300$ beside it. Two engines, one training pool, two different
constants for one rule. GEPA also lifted from
\texttt{rel-007-capacity-scale}; SkillOpt did not, and 63 could not have come
from that template, whose own variable is drawn from
\NUM{\rangeRelZeroZeroSevenCapacityScaleHeadroompctLow} to
\NUM{\rangeRelZeroZeroSevenCapacityScaleHeadroompctHigh}.

The generalisation is weaker than the pair suggests and worth stating at its
real strength. Six frozen winners were on disk across this project's searches
when we counted, and none of those directories survives
(\Cref{sec:limitations}). Three
carry constants lifted from single training items and three carry none, so this
is a common outcome of an accept-if-strictly-better ratchet with no held-out
check, not a certain one. Neither engine was asked to memorise and neither
mechanism is a defect in the engines as implemented.

\paragraph{What each engine accepted its winner on.} SkillOpt scored all nine
of its candidates on all 21 validation items: seed 18, winner 21. GEPA scored
its generation-zero candidate on 21 items and every candidate after it on three.
Its \texttt{winner.json} closes at score $1.000$ over $n = 3$, with
\texttt{winner\_source = engine}: the search ran to completion, so GEPA's own
acceptance rule chose, and it chose on three items answered correctly. GEPA's
$1.000$ and its seed's $0.762$ are therefore not the same measurement and cannot
be differenced. This is not a defect in our harness either. A
\texttt{\_best\_validated} path exists and picks a body scored on the whole pool,
and it is used only when a search is budget-stopped. An engine's acceptance rule
is part of what the engine is, and this study tests what an engine hands you.
What GEPA handed us is a document selected on three items that then lost to an
empty prompt on \NUM{\unseenItems}.
