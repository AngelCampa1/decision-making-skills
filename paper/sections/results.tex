\subsection{Control tokens and parse validity}
\label{sec:parsefail}

Every arm's unreadable answers are scored wrong. That is the study's
convention and it is defensible, but it makes accuracy a sum of decision
quality and format compliance. In an earlier pilot of this evaluation,
Qwen3 echoed its thinking-mode switch (\texttt{/think} or \texttt{/no\_think})
after its answers (e.g., \texttt{ANSWER: monitor /think}), which an unrepaired
scorer refused as an option not on the menu.

To eliminate this verifier defect, the study ran with a pre-parsing strip step
that removes trailing control switches before matching against option menus.
Across all \NUM{\studyReadings} arm readings in this study, trailing control tokens
appeared on 48 calls (0.70\%). With the strip step active, all 48 parsed cleanly,
yielding zero parse rejections from control tokens. The registered scores and
the post-hoc re-read scores are therefore identical across all seven arms,
confirming that parse failure was not a confound in this evaluation.

\subsection{What accuracy hides}
\label{sec:signal}

Discrimination and skew are recomputed directly from the trial records above
without additional model calls. Because each record pairs the ground truth key
with the model response, option choice distributions can be separated into
discrimination ability and positional preference.

\paragraph{The measure.} Accuracy on a two-option key adds together two
different quantities: how well a model tells the cases apart, and how often it
picks one option whatever the case. Write accuracy as
$\pi \cdot \mathrm{sens} + (1 - \pi) \cdot \mathrm{spec}$ for a base rate
$\pi$. At $\pi = 0.5$ that collapses to $(J + 1)/2$, a function of
informedness alone, where informedness is Youden's
$J = \mathrm{sensitivity} + \mathrm{specificity} - 1$: zero for any
constant-answer policy at any base rate, unmoved by a pure shift in
preference, and symmetric in which option is called positive. Every template
in this corpus is balanced by construction, and the identity holds in our
records to the fourth decimal in every cell where no answer went unread. So on
this corpus response bias contributes nothing to accuracy, and the case for
reporting $J$ rests on two other things it exposes: which templates carry any
discrimination at all, and what happens when parse failure breaks the
identity by landing unevenly on the key (\Cref{sec:parsefail}). \emph{Skew},
the model's rate for an option minus the rate the items wanted it, names the
bias half directly; we report the mean of its absolute value over templates.

We compute both per template and average over templates, so no template's item
count decides the answer. Rows the model did not parse are excluded, a
limitation \Cref{sec:limitations} carries. A template whose key holds a single
answer class has no informedness at all, and the implementation refuses to
return a value for it: recording an undefined cell as zero would put it into a
mean as though it were a measurement of no discrimination.

\subsection{What the engines actually produced}
\label{sec:whatengines}

The scores above are the answer to the registered question. What the winners
contain is a separate finding and does not depend on a score.

\paragraph{Provenance.} Both winners' bodies are committed in full in
\pth{results/evolution/}: GEPA's frozen winner (\texttt{f7589ca4\ldots}) and
SkillOpt's frozen winner (\texttt{7a2e2784\ldots}), alongside their search logs,
lineages, and configurations. The figures describing how the winners then
\emph{scored} are macros generated directly from the study's committed records.

\textbf{Both winners committed to disk exhibit explicit training-item content
lifted directly into the skill body.}

GEPA's winner carries an instruction specifically tuned to warranty claims,
quoting specific ticket identifiers (\texttt{ticket 40078}), warranty clauses
(``\texttt{Alder covers this model for 23 months}''), and elapsed months
(\texttt{22 months}) verbatim from single training items in
\texttt{hrd-001-warranty-claim}.

SkillOpt's winner incorporates an automated numeric threshold evaluation
procedure with hardcoded worked examples quoting exact numerical cutoffs from
training templates: Warranty (\texttt{22 < 23} months), Shipping
(\texttt{10 > 4} days late), and Loan Review (score \texttt{737 >= 665}), as well
as cache-tier scaling clauses from \texttt{rel-007-capacity-scale}.
Neither engine was asked to memorise: both were tasked with improving scores on
training items, and lifting constants and worked examples from those items is an
effective path for an accept-if-strictly-better ratchet.

\paragraph{What each engine accepted its winner on.} SkillOpt evaluated candidates
on all 21 items of the validation pool: its winning candidate scored 21 of 21 (1.000).
GEPA's search log records that it likewise evaluated and selected its winning candidate
on the full 21-item validation pool, achieving 1.000 ($21/21$). Both engines thus
accepted their frozen winners on what appeared to be perfect validation performance,
yet neither winner produced a generalizable advantage over a matched placebo
when evaluated across holdout scenarios.

\paragraph{The constant ablation probe.} To test whether the seen-set scores were
sustained by these transcribed constants rather than general procedural rules, we
evaluated ablated variants of both winners across all 392 seen items under
identical conditions (784 calls, \pth{datasets/ablations/}). The ablated variants
preserved section structure and word count (within 1\%) while abstracting worked-example
numbers and scenario constants into symbolic notation (e.g., $S \ge T$ for Loan Review)
and replacing unconditional positive answers with balanced conditional logic.
On \texttt{rel-010-loan-review}, where SkillOpt quoted \texttt{threshold 665, score 737},
abstracting to general notation dropped accuracy from 0.9286 (52 of 56) to 0.8214
(46 of 56), falling below its matched placebo (0.8571).
On \texttt{hrd-002-shipping-escalation}, removing GEPA's unconditional warranty recommendation
dropped accuracy from 0.8036 to 0.5714 (a 23.2 percentage point loss). Across all 392 seen
items, both ablated variants (0.8597 for GEPA, 0.8367 for SkillOpt) remained below the
unprompted baseline (\texttt{off}: 0.8673).
