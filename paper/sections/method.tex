\subsection{Design}

Five arms over the same items, paired by item. Only the system prompt differs.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
arm & system prompt \\
\midrule
\texttt{off} & task framing and response-format contract only \\
\texttt{on} & control plus the hand-written seed skill \\
\texttt{placebo} & control plus word- and structure-matched filler \\
\texttt{gepa} & control plus GEPA's frozen winner \\
\texttt{skillopt} & control plus SkillOpt's frozen winner \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Three invariants make this a comparison rather than a demonstration, and their
absence is what makes most skill evaluations uninterpretable.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{The response-format contract is in every arm.} A skill that
    also teaches the model how to format an answer, tested against a control
    that was never told, measures instruction-following.
  \item \textbf{The placebo is matched on word count and on structure.} It is
    the control every comparison is made against. \texttt{off} is reported
    alongside, outside the family, because it answers a different question:
    whether any document helps at all. The match is \NUM{\placeboWords} words
    against the skill's \NUM{\skillWords} and \NUM{\placeboSections} headings
    against \NUM{\skillSections}. It is \emph{not} matched against the evolved
    winners, whose lengths the engines chose, and
    \Cref{sec:length-confound} gives the delivered sizes and what they cost.
  \item \textbf{The option menu is held constant across arms.} Removing option
    menus moved trajectory accuracy by 14 to 40 points in
    AgentAtlas~\citep{agentatlas2026}, which is larger than any effect we
    expected to measure.
\end{enumerate}

All five arms are built by one function, \texttt{solvers.arms.build\_arm}, and
told apart in the records by the SHA-256 of the body each carries. An arm that
silently received the wrong body would be a different content hash in the
record.

\subsection{What the placebo controls, and what it does not}
\label{sec:length-confound}

The placebo is matched to the seed skill. It is not matched to the evolved
winners, whose length each engine chose, and the gap is large enough to report
in the method rather than the limitations.

Every record carries \texttt{input\_tokens}. The \texttt{off} arm sends the task
framing and the format contract and no document, so its prompt is the shared
prefix and each other arm's document is the difference. Per item that difference
has zero variance, so these are exact sizes and not estimates.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
arm & body tokens & $\times$ placebo \\
\midrule
\texttt{placebo} & \NUM{\bodyPlaceboTokens} & \NUM{\bodyPlaceboRatio} \\
\texttt{on} & \NUM{\bodyOnTokens} & \NUM{\bodyOnRatio} \\
\texttt{gepa} & \NUM{\bodyGepaTokens} & \NUM{\bodyGepaRatio} \\
\texttt{skillopt} & \NUM{\bodySkilloptTokens} & \NUM{\bodySkilloptRatio} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

So the comparison this paper is named after puts a
\NUM{\bodySkilloptTokens}-token document against a
\NUM{\bodyPlaceboTokens}-token one. Where an evolved winner beats the placebo,
length is not controlled and the length reading and the content reading cannot
be told apart. Where it loses, as GEPA does on the unseen set, length is not the
available explanation, since the longer document is the one that lost.

A placebo generated per treatment arm would close this. That is a design we did
not run, and no analysis of these records recovers it.

\subsection{Arenas, and why this study emits no verdict}
\label{sec:arenas}

The harness registers every model against one of three tiers. \texttt{dev} is
for development and emits no verdict. \texttt{screen} is for cheap hosted models
and may. \texttt{confirm} is the target model on a private holdout under a
hash-locked pre-registration.

The separation is enforced in code rather than by discipline, and it cuts
against us here. Our target, \NUM{\studyModel}, is registered \texttt{dev}. This
study therefore produces a published, controlled, reproducible result that is
formally scoped to one small model and moves nothing on the project's scorecard.
We report it that way throughout. \Cref{sec:ceiling} explains why a
\texttt{screen}-tier replication does not exist.

\subsection{Pre-registration, verified rather than asserted}

The prediction for this study was committed to the repository's append-only
notebook before the first call. What makes that more than a claim is a gate: a
published run declares the notebook entry carrying its prediction, and the
provenance check refuses the run unless that entry's first commit is a git
ancestor of the commit the run was made at. Ancestry is not editable after the
fact without destroying the timestamps the method rests on, and the repository
refuses commits under any address but one.

The registered family was fixed before any data existed: three comparisons,
\texttt{on}, \texttt{gepa} and \texttt{skillopt}, each against \texttt{placebo},
one-sided in the direction of the arm helping. \texttt{off} sits outside the
family by registration rather than by convenience.

One amendment was appended mid-run, after 143 of \NUM{\studyReadings} calls and
before any comparison was read: the corpus contains no memorisable constant, so
the mechanism one prediction rested on could not occur. The amendment is dated
and in the record, and the prediction it weakened is still there saying so.

\subsection{Statistics}

The registered comparison is McNemar's exact test on the discordant pairs,
item-matched, one-sided, with Holm applied across the registered family of
three. The two item sets are analysed separately and never pooled.

That test treats items as independent, and they are not. Items minted from one
template share a scenario, a rule and a distractor pool, so the independent unit
is the template. The registration named the item-matched test and we report it
as registered, but it is the wrong unit and we report a template-level test
beside it throughout (\Cref{sec:clustered}). Intervals come from a cluster
bootstrap that resamples \emph{templates}, not items, for the same reason.

Choosing the unit after seeing the data would be the defect this design exists
to avoid, so both are reported everywhere and neither is presented as the
headline. Where they disagree, they disagree in the direction of the clustered
test being the more conservative, and \Cref{sec:clustered} says by how much.

We report the discordant split beside every $p$-value. Two arms can land on
identical accuracy while disagreeing on a third of the items, and an aggregate
alone cannot tell agreement from cancellation.

\subsection{Controls run before the results were read}

\paragraph{A/A.} The placebo was scored a second time over all
\NUM{\totalItems} items, into a separate checkpoint so that resume could not
skip the pass and report perfect agreement by construction.

\paragraph{Falsifier.} The \texttt{off} arm returns a non-zero score, so some
possible response scored above zero in every arm and the scorer reads the same
object in each. This project has shipped four estimators that could not return a
non-zero value, each producing a clean run and a plausible number, which is why
the check is standing rather than occasional.

\paragraph{Residency.} Model residency is pinned at 60 minutes on every request.
A probe the day before the study found two items answering deterministically to
whether the model had just been loaded, which would have put a venue artifact
inside the A/A. The pin is a property of the provider rather than of the run
manifest, which \Cref{sec:appendix-harness} states as the gap it is.

\paragraph{Arm ordering.} Arms ran in blocks rather than interleaved by item.
That is a design defect, not a choice, and \Cref{sec:limitations} reports what
bounds it.
