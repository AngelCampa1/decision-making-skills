The run reported here was conducted under Version 1 of the standing protocol.
Version 2 added two corpus-admission criteria on 2026-08-28, after the run,

\subsection{Design}

Five arms over the same items, paired by item. Only the system prompt differs.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
arm & system prompt \\
\midrule
\texttt{off} & task framing and response-format contract only \\
\texttt{on} & control plus the hand-written seed skill \\
\texttt{placebo} & control plus word- and structure-matched filler \\
\texttt{gepa} & control plus GEPA's frozen winner \\
\texttt{skillopt} & control plus SkillOpt's frozen winner \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Three invariants hold across every arm.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{The response-format contract is in every arm.} A skill that
    also teaches the model how to format an answer, tested against a control
    that was never told, measures instruction-following.
  \item \textbf{The placebo is matched on word count and on structure.} It is
    the control every comparison is made against. \texttt{off} is reported
    alongside, outside the family, because it answers a different question:
    whether any document helps at all. The match is \NUM{\placeboWords} words
    against the skill's \NUM{\skillWords} and \NUM{\placeboSections} headings
    against \NUM{\skillSections}. It is \emph{not} matched against the evolved
    winners, whose lengths the engines chose, and
    \Cref{sec:length-confound} gives the delivered sizes and what they cost.
  \item \textbf{The option menu is held constant across arms.} Removing the
    explicit label menu moved trajectory accuracy by 14 to 40 points in
    AgentAtlas v1~\citep{agentatlas2026}. We cite v1 because v2 withdrew the
    number and replaced it with ``mapped label agreement can change
    substantially'', alongside a caution against reading its synthetic study
    as a model comparison. The figure therefore justifies holding the menu
    constant and nothing else; we do not compare it to our own effects.
\end{enumerate}

All five arms are built by one function, \texttt{solvers.arms.build\_arm}, and
told apart in the records by the SHA-256 of the body each carries. An arm that
silently received the wrong body would be a different content hash in the
record.

\subsection{What the placebo controls, and what it does not}
\label{sec:length-confound}

The placebo is matched to the seed skill. It is not matched to the evolved
winners, whose length each engine chose, and the gap is large enough to report
here in the method.

Every record carries \texttt{input\_tokens}. The \texttt{off} arm sends the task
framing and the format contract and no document, so its prompt is the shared
prefix and each other arm's document is the difference. Per item that difference
has zero variance, so these are exact sizes.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
arm & body tokens & $\times$ placebo \\
\midrule
\texttt{placebo} & \NUM{\bodyPlaceboTokens} & \NUM{\bodyPlaceboRatio} \\
\texttt{on} & \NUM{\bodyOnTokens} & \NUM{\bodyOnRatio} \\
\texttt{gepa} & \NUM{\bodyGepaTokens} & \NUM{\bodyGepaRatio} \\
\texttt{skillopt} & \NUM{\bodySkilloptTokens} & \NUM{\bodySkilloptRatio} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

So the comparison this paper is named after puts a
\NUM{\bodySkilloptTokens}-token document against a
\NUM{\bodyPlaceboTokens}-token one. Where an evolved winner beats the placebo,
length is not controlled and the length reading and the content reading cannot
be told apart. Where it loses, as GEPA does on the unseen set, length is not the
available explanation, since the longer document is the one that lost.

A placebo generated per treatment arm would close this. That is a design we did
not run, and no analysis of these records recovers it.

\subsection{Scope of the verdict}
\label{sec:arenas}

The project that ran this study registers every model against a tier that
decides whether a run on it may support a public claim about the shipped
skill, and the study's target, \NUM{\studyModel}, is registered at the
development tier. The result here is therefore published and controlled, and
scoped to one small model; it supports no claim about the skill at large.
\Cref{sec:noreplication} explains why a larger-model replication does not
exist.

\subsection{Pre-registration, verified rather than asserted}

The prediction for this study was committed to the repository's append-only
notebook before the first call. What makes that more than a claim is a gate: a
published run declares the notebook entry carrying its prediction, and the
provenance check refuses the run unless that entry's first commit is a git
ancestor of the commit the run was made at. Ancestry is not editable after the
fact without destroying the timestamps the method rests on.

The registered family was fixed before any data existed: three comparisons,
\texttt{on}, \texttt{gepa} and \texttt{skillopt}, each against \texttt{placebo},
one-sided in the direction of the arm helping, applied to each item set
separately. That is a family of three per set and six registered comparisons in
all, and the two sets are never pooled. \texttt{off} sits outside the family by
registration.

One amendment was appended to the prediction entry. Its text says it was
written after 143 of \NUM{\studyCalls} calls and before any comparison was
read. Its commit, \texttt{03f4df8}, was made seven hours after the run's
commit \texttt{53b4965} and in the same commit as the results write-up, so the
ancestry the gate checks places it after the data, and its timing rests on the
entry's own word. What it says: the corpus fixes no constant \emph{across
items}, because every threshold is redrawn per item and restated in the
prompt, so the mechanism one prediction rested on could not occur
(\Cref{sec:corpus}). The prediction it weakened is the one that then failed,
and \Cref{sec:predictions} scores it as registered.

\subsection{Statistics}

The registered comparison is McNemar's exact test on the discordant pairs,
item-matched, one-sided, with Holm applied across the registered family of
three within each item set.

That test treats items as independent, and they are not. Items minted from one
template share a scenario, a rule and a distractor pool, so the independent
unit is the template. The registration named the item-matched test and we
report it as registered, but it is the wrong unit and we report a
template-level test beside it in \Cref{sec:clustered}. The informedness
intervals of \Cref{tab:signal} come from a cluster bootstrap that resamples
\emph{templates} for the same reason; the two accuracy tables carry no interval
at all, which \Cref{sec:results} records as a gap.

Choosing the unit after seeing the data would be the defect this design exists
to avoid, so both are reported and neither is presented as the headline. They
do not disagree in one direction: on the seen set the clustered test is the
more conservative throughout, and on the unseen set two of the three go the
other way, \texttt{gepa} furthest, from $p = \NUM{\pUnseenGepa}$ to
\NUM{\clusteredUnseenGepa}. \Cref{sec:clustered} gives both columns.

We report the discordant split beside every registered $p$-value. Two arms can
land on identical accuracy while disagreeing on a third of the items, and an
aggregate alone cannot tell agreement from cancellation.

\subsection{Controls run before the results were read}

\paragraph{A/A.} The placebo was scored a second time over all
\NUM{\totalItems} items, into a separate checkpoint so that resume could not
skip the pass and report perfect agreement by construction. \Cref{sec:results}
reports the outcome.

\paragraph{Falsifier.} A standing rule of the protocol, checked by hand
before any comparison was read: some possible response must score above zero
for every arm, and the scorer must read the same object in each. The rule
exists because an estimator that cannot return a non-zero value produces a
clean run and a plausible number, and nothing else about the run looks
wrong. \Cref{sec:results} reports the observation that satisfies it.

\paragraph{Residency.} Model residency is pinned at 60 minutes on every
request. A probe run hours before the study found two items answering
deterministically to whether the model had just been loaded, which without the
pin would have put a venue artifact inside the A/A control. The pin is a
property of the provider, and \Cref{sec:appendix-harness} states which other
settings share that status.

\paragraph{Arm ordering.} Arms ran in blocks, every item of one arm and then
every item of the next. We intended item-major interleaving and the study path
does not do it. \Cref{sec:limitations} reports the exposure and what bounds
it.
