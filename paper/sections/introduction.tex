\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Markdown ``skills'' are a real distribution channel. The Agent Skills
standard fixes a \texttt{SKILL.md} format and a shared
\texttt{.agents/skills/} discovery path, and its own client showcase listed
forty-six adopting products in August 2026, among them Claude Code, Codex,
Cursor, GitHub Copilot, VS Code, Gemini CLI and
Amp~\citep{agentskills2025}. These files are installed and running inside
coding agents today. Almost none of them carry evidence that they help.

A second layer has grown on top of the first. Engines now rewrite skill
files automatically: GEPA proposes revised instructions from reflection over
rollouts~\citep{gepa2026}, and Microsoft's SkillOpt makes bounded edits to a
skill body and keeps any edit that scores strictly
better~\citep{skillopt2026}. Both accept an edit on a single unreplicated
score comparison, with no notion of a difference that could have arisen by
chance.

This paper is about the two controls that acceptance rule is missing, and
about what happens when they are added. The first is a placebo: no engine in
this space compares its output against a document of matched length and
structure whose content says nothing, so a gain from an evolved skill and a
gain from any structured text in the system prompt are indistinguishable in
every published number. The second is power arithmetic computed from the
design before the run: how small a $p$-value the design can produce at all,
and how large an effect it could detect. Both are cheap, and this study is
evidence for both: we ran it with the placebo in place and computed the
arithmetic only afterwards.

\paragraph{What we did.}
We held the model fixed, evolved a decision-making skill with each engine
against a training split, froze both winners by content hash, and then
minted a fresh holdout the engines had never seen. Five arms ran over that
holdout: no skill, the hand-written seed skill, a placebo matched to that
skill on word count and structure, and the two evolved winners, whose
lengths the engines chose and which the placebo therefore does not match
(\Cref{sec:length-confound}). Every comparison is McNemar's exact test
against the placebo, paired by item, with Holm correction across a family of
three per item set, six registered comparisons in all, fixed before the
first call. The prediction was committed to git before any data existed, and
the run's provenance gate refuses a result whose prediction cannot be shown
by ancestry to predate it.

\paragraph{What we found.}
No arm rejects, on either item set, after correction. The best showing is
SkillOpt's winner at $\NUM{\effectSeenSkillopt}$ on the scenarios it trained
against, which does not survive multiplicity. On scenarios neither engine
saw, GEPA's winner falls furthest below an empty prompt, and the skill we
wrote falls below the placebo too. Both winners had written training-item
constants into their own bodies, each asserting a different threshold for
the same rule on a variable the corpus redraws for every item. GEPA accepted
its winner on three items, by its own acceptance rule, and never scored it
on its validation pool.

A null is only worth reading if the study could have returned something
else, so we checked, and half of it could not have. Our items come from
templates, and the template is the independent unit. Under that unit the
unseen set is three clusters, a one-sided test over three clusters cannot
return a $p$ below \NUM{\floorUnseen}, and all three of our registered
unseen comparisons were therefore unfalsifiable at any outcome. The seen set
survives the same check but its headline does not: SkillOpt's
$p = \NUM{\pSeenSkillopt}$ becomes \NUM{\clusteredSeenSkillopt} under a
template-level sign-flip test. The pre-registration had computed minimum
detectable effects of \NUM{\mdeUnseen} unseen and \NUM{\mdeSeen} seen, the
first already above anything the study found, and computed both at a design
effect of 1.0; at the clustering our own protocol specifies they rise to
\NUM{\mdeClusteredUnseen} and \NUM{\mdeClusteredSeen}, and the smaller is
\NUM{\mdeRatio} times the largest gain the study went on to observe. Every
one of those numbers was available from the registration before a single
call was made.

The same records also say something about the instrument. Pooled accuracy
hides which templates discriminate, and the reported metric, which counts an
unreadable answer as wrong, does not even order them: a template that is
always right when it answers reports below one that is mostly guessing.
Computing discrimination per template left the study's conclusion standing
and put \NUM{\lowSignalTemplates} of our \NUM{\studyTemplates} templates
below $J = \NUM{\lowSignalThreshold}$ in every arm, so a
\NUM{\totalItems}-item run carries the discriminative signal of
\NUM{\signalItems}.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
% Each of these stands whether the engines succeeded or failed. That is
% deliberate: a contribution contingent on a positive result is a bet.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{The first placebo-controlled evaluation of automated skill
    optimisation we are aware of}, with an A/A determinism control reported
    beside it. The placebo is not inert: it beats the skill we wrote on both
    item sets, and on the seen set it beats the empty prompt by more than
    any registered gain in the study (\Cref{sec:results}).
  \item \textbf{The arithmetic showing our own registered design was
    undecidable, computable from the registration alone.} A one-sided test over
    \NUM{\templatesUnseen} template clusters has a $p$-floor of
    \NUM{\floorUnseen}, so three of our six primaries could not have
    rejected at any result; and the minimum detectable effect at our
    protocol's own design effect exceeds the largest observed gain by
    \NUM{\mdeRatio} times. Both checks are two lines of arithmetic over a
    registration, and either would have stopped this study
    (\Cref{sec:clustered}, \Cref{sec:limitations}).
  \item \textbf{A discrimination measure reported beside accuracy}, applied
    to our own published records, showing that three templates measure
    nothing in any arm and that the published metric does not order the
    others by what they measure (\Cref{sec:signal}).
  \item \textbf{Both winners memorised, and both were accepted on evidence
    too thin to notice.} Two engines, one training pool, two different
    constants written into the skill body for the same redrawn-per-item
    rule; one winner accepted on a score over three items
    (\Cref{sec:whatengines}). A pre-study probe anticipated exactly this
    channel, and the holdout was rebuilt to catch it before either engine
    ran (\Cref{sec:corpus}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Scope, stated once and up front.}
This is one target model, one corpus, one harness. The target is a 1.7B
parameter local model, and every larger model we later screened solves the
corpus with an empty prompt, so no replication above that tier exists
(\Cref{sec:noreplication}). The claim is that on this model, under these
controls, neither engine's output beat a placebo. Whether it would on a
frontier model is open, and the screen that says why is itself a result:
every hosted model we measured solves this corpus with an empty prompt
(\Cref{sec:noreplication}).
