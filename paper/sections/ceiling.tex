\Cref{sec:noreplication} states the outcome: this study cannot be replicated
on a model large enough to carry a verdict, because the corpus has no
headroom on one. This appendix reports the evidence and what the repair
attempt produced.

Provenance first, because it is mixed. The model screen is published beside
the study's records as a committed artifact,
\pth{nvbuild-ceiling-screen.json}, holding \NUM{\screenModels} models, and
its figures are generated macros like any other. The nine-template work after
it ran as probes through sub-agents rather than through the checkpointed
runner, so those passes wrote no records, and their figures are typed here
from the dated notebook entries that registered each prediction before its
probe ran. The admission-rule subsection turns the criteria on the study's
own corpus, so its skew and informedness figures come from the five-arm
study's committed records; they are typed because \texttt{de figures} emits a
per-template informedness table (\Cref{tab:templates} prints the same
values) and no per-template skew macro.

\subsection{Every hosted model we measured solves the corpus}

Screened with an empty prompt on \NUM{\screenItems} items each, every model
in the artifact answers this corpus at or near ceiling:
\NUM{\screenAtCeiling} of \NUM{\screenModels} at \NUM{\screenBest} and the
weakest at \NUM{\screenWorst}, against \NUM{\accUnseenOff} for the study's
local target. Accuracy there is over answered items, and
\NUM{\screenUnanswered} call across the whole screen returned nothing, so one
of the perfect scores is over one item fewer. Eleven models the screen's key
can reach fall under the \texttt{nvbuild/} namespace, a free-tier hosted
endpoint whose vendor prefixes are registered in the harness; eight returned
inside the screen's budget, and one of those
eight is recorded in the notebook entry and missing from the committed file.
We report the file's count, because a record that gets edited to match a
paper is not a record. Three reachable registered models did not return inside the budget and are
recorded as unmeasured; they are larger than models already answering at
\NUM{\screenBest}, so the expectation is clear and it is an expectation.

A five-arm study needs headroom for an arm to move into. At
\NUM{\screenWorst} there is not enough for any effect this design could
detect, so no venue we screened can host this study as the corpus stands. The
screen covered one of the three backends registered at that tier, which
bounds what this rules out.

\subsection{Nine harder templates, and how they failed}

We authored nine harder templates in one sitting, across seven domains; two
of the nine were built to diagnose a third.
Eight are built around a rule the model has to select before it can apply, a
precedence between two thresholds chosen by a categorical variable stated
elsewhere in the item. \texttt{hrd-001-warranty-claim} is the exception and
carries a single threshold with no selector. The probes ran on
\texttt{nvidia/nemotron-3-nano-30b-a3b}, the 30B model from the screen above.

Three predictions were registered, each before its probe, and each was
falsified.

\emph{First: a set of six selection templates would land the 30B between
0.60 and 0.75.} The set landed at 0.949, and five of the six sit at or
beside 1.000.

\emph{Second: an arm that gains accuracy on the one template that held the
model off the ceiling would gain it by moving skew.} That template read 0.722
on the eighteen items of its second pass, and it turned out to be one-sided
rather than hard. Measured three times on the item type, at 0.629 on 35
items, 0.583 on 24, and 0.625 on 24 items of a reversed variant, it reaches
each number with sensitivity 1.000 against specificity of 0.235, 0.167 and
0.250: the model answers one option on about nine items in ten, in a set that
wants it on half. That is an informedness of 0.167 to 0.250, and on a
balanced key accuracy is pinned at $(J+1)/2$, which is exactly the two
balanced measurements, 0.583 and 0.625; the first pass ran on a realised
18/17 split, where the identity gives 0.618 against the 0.629 observed. A
nudge toward the other option would move both error rates and leave $J$
where it is, so no arm could gain from it. The premise never occurred: over
36 items and three arms, no skill and the placebo read 0.694 and 0.697, and
the seed skill read 0.600. That third arm is the one case in this project
where a skill visibly traded one property for another: it cut skew from
$+0.306$ to $+0.171$ and cut informedness from 0.389 to 0.190 at the same
time, a smaller lean bought with less discrimination, at a net loss of 0.094
accuracy. Every interval crosses zero at 36 items.

\emph{Third: reversing which option is named first would move accuracy by
more than 0.10.} It moved 0.042, so the bias is semantic rather than
positional, and its cause is open. A companion probe that added two
high-strength money facts to a ceilinged template, to invite weighing cost
instead of applying the rule, changed nothing: 23 of 23 against a control's
23 of 23.

\subsection{The admission rule this bought}

``Make the corpus harder'' is not a specification, and following it produced
nine templates that were either easy or one-sided. Three criteria replace it,
all computable from the control-arm records a calibration run already
produces: accuracy below the ceiling, so an arm has somewhere to move; skew
near zero, so the model is answering from the item; and informedness below
one, so discrimination is left to gain.

Seven published templates and six of the nine hard ones have signal and no
room, on a large model. The two one-sided ones have room and no signal.
\texttt{hrd-001} has signal and no room either, at 24 of 24 on the 30B, and
it is the one template of the nine built without a selector.

\textbf{The three criteria do not close the case, which we found by turning
them on our own corpus.} \texttt{rel-009-flight-rebook} passes all three on
the empty-prompt arm: reported accuracy
\NUM{\accUnseenRelZeroZeroNineFlightRebookOff}, inside the band and far
below any ceiling; skew of $+0.019$, nearer zero than every template in the
corpus but the two that read exactly $0.000$; and informedness of $+0.028$,
below one. It is also one of the three templates \Cref{sec:signal} marks as
measuring nothing. A coin has zero skew and zero informedness too. The third
criterion bounds discrimination above and not below, and a lower bound is
the obvious repair, but we have not run a corpus against one and will not
present an untested rule as a result. What this appendix delivers is a
criterion set that is necessary and demonstrably not sufficient.

A fourth criterion, that both options should be acts so neither is the
default a model reaches for under uncertainty, is stated as a hypothesis and
the nearest testable version of it did not hold on our data. Signed toward
the act on the 1.7B, six act-or-wait templates average $+0.097$, carried
almost entirely by one template at $+0.464$; three two-act templates average
absolute skew of 0.172, higher rather than lower.
\texttt{rel-005-security-patch} resists the classification, since
\texttt{patch\_immediately} against \texttt{schedule\_patch} is two acts
under one reading and an act against a deferral under the other, and it
carries the second-largest skew magnitude in the corpus at $-0.400$.
Putting it in the first group takes $+0.097$ to $+0.026$; putting it in the
second takes 0.172 to 0.229. The direction of the comparison survives
either placement and the two averages do not. The action-bias explanation
is refuted on our data and the underlying cause is open.

\subsection{What would unblock a replication}

Three options are open and none is chosen. A corpus of more than two options
per item removes the ceiling confound at the cost of a harder scoring path
and a weaker pairing. A multi-step task changes what is being measured. And a
genuinely hard rule-selection template may exist that we failed to write,
which nine failures make less likely without ruling out. What we will not do
is tune a template until a model finds it hard: that procedure produces a
corpus fitted to one model's weaknesses, and the scoping rule of
\Cref{sec:arenas} exists to stop exactly that reading.
