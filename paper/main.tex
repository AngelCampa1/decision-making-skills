\begin{abstract}
Two open-source engines, GEPA and Microsoft's SkillOpt, automatically rewrite
the markdown ``skill'' files that agent tools load, and both accept an edit on a
single uncontrolled score improvement. We evolved a decision-making skill with
each engine on one corpus of computable-ground-truth decisions, against one
target model, \NUM{\studyModel}, inside one harness. We then tested five arms
against each other on a holdout minted after both winners were frozen: no skill, the hand-written seed skill, a
structure-matched placebo, and the two evolved winners. Comparisons are McNemar
exact against the placebo with Holm correction across a pre-registered family of
\NUM{\familySize}, on \NUM{\unseenItems} and \NUM{\seenItems} items analysed separately.

\textbf{No arm rejects on either item set.} The best showing is SkillOpt at
$\NUM{\effectSeenSkillopt}$ on trained scenarios, raw $p = \NUM{\pSeenSkillopt}$,
which Holm takes to \NUM{\qSeenSkillopt}. On scenarios neither engine saw,
GEPA's winner scores \NUM{\accUnseenGepa} against \NUM{\accUnseenOff} for an
empty prompt: worse than no skill at all, though that deficit is one template of
three and reverses on the other two. An A/A control returned \NUM{\aaPairs} of
\NUM{\aaPairs} items identical, which bounds the drift the arms' block ordering
exposes them to without removing it. Both winners had written training-item
constants into their own bodies, and GEPA accepted its winner on three items.

\textbf{The more useful result is that we can show the design could not have
answered its own question.} Items are drawn from templates and the template is
the independent unit, so the unseen set is three clusters, and three clusters
admit no one-sided $p$ below \NUM{\floorUnseen}. All three unseen primaries were
unfalsifiable before the first call. The pre-registration had computed a minimum
detectable effect of \NUM{\mdeUnseen} assuming no clustering; at the design
effect our own protocol specifies it is \NUM{\mdeClusteredUnseen}, and the
smaller of the two clustered figures is still \NUM{\mdeRatio} times the largest
gain the study went on to observe. Both
numbers were computable from the registration alone. Neither was carried into
the paper until this version, and we think that omission, not the null, is the
transferable finding.

A second measurement result concerns the instrument. Pooled accuracy hides
which templates discriminate, and the metric this study reports, which counts an
unreadable answer as wrong, does not even order them: one template answered
perfectly whenever it was answered reports below one that is mostly guessing.
Reporting discrimination directly leaves the headline standing and shows
\NUM{\lowSignalTemplates} of \NUM{\studyTemplates} templates below
$J = \NUM{\lowSignalThreshold}$ in every arm, two of them indistinguishably
from zero, so a \NUM{\totalItems}-item run carries the signal of
\NUM{\signalItems}. We argue both failure modes are generic to the
two-option, template-built evaluations
this literature is built on.
\end{abstract}
